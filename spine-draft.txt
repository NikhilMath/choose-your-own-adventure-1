KEYBOARD HEIST: SPINE DRAFT (v8, 35 scenes)
Status: UNAPPROVED PROPOSAL. Nothing here is in the game yet. Change anything.
Target length: about 20 scenes, more if we like where it goes. [YOURS]
Rules: single choices only (each scene ends with one choice to the next scene),
no forks, no diversions. Diversions come after the spine is approved.
Shape: grounded office comedy that gets weirder and weirder, peaks almost in
space, then drops back to grounded reality for the ending. [YOURS]

Source key: [YOURS] = direction the story creator gave. [PROPOSED] = Claude's idea.
Every scene aims to stay short, a few terse beats, per the README.

==============================================================================
ACT 1: THE ITCH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. ARRIVAL  (written, approved)
   Plateau paragraph, Daniel's 4:46 p.m. printer-driver ticket.
   Choice: "Head to his office."

2. OFFICE  (written earlier, commit 5e5552f; to be rebuilt without the fork)
   Nice-CEO Daniel: open door, granola bar, Bible verse Post-it, kids' photo. He
   steps out for a call. You type on his keyboard and notice a keyboard for the
   first time in 22 years. [YOURS: setup / details from the earlier version]
   Choice [PROPOSED]: "Finish the driver. Leave it where it is. For now."

3. THE DRIVE HOME  [PROPOSED]
   The printer works. You go home. Dinner, TV, nothing, same as always, except
   your fingers keep typing on the air. It's the first thing in months you're
   thinking about.
   Choice: "Look it up."

4. THE MODEL NUMBER  [PROPOSED]
   You find the exact keyboard online. $149.99. Ordinary, which is the problem.
   Nothing about it explains what you felt.
   Choice: "Buy one."

5. THE IMPOSTOR  [YOURS: premise / PROPOSED: scene]
   It arrives. You type on it at the kitchen table. Nothing. Same model, same
   keys, dead. Buying it doesn't work; it has to be his. Now you have a decoy.
   Choice: "Bring it to work."

ACT 2: THE PLAN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE PLAN  [PROPOSED]
   Next morning. You are early. You buy your own coffee. You start a spreadsheet.
   Purpose, for the first time in months, and it's a crime.
   Choice: "Case the office."

7. RECON  [PROPOSED]
   Daniel's schedule. His lunch. His door that is always open. His assistant,
   Marcy, who sees everything. Her calendar has the company retreat next Friday,
   and Daniel brings his own keyboard to everything. Marcy calls it "the founding
   keyboard": he started the company on it. Once it leaves the building, it's gone.
   (Seed for later: a flyer on the break room fridge, "BIG NEWS COMING," and
   Daniel humming.)
   Choice: "Wait for a window."

8. THE WINDOW  [PROPOSED]
   Daniel leaves for a meeting. The office is empty. You get as far as the door.
   Marcy looks up. You pretend to check a cable. You leave.
   Choice: "Get everyone out of the building."

9. THE OUTAGE  [YOURS: internet outage / PROPOSED: details; merges the old outage and Marcy's errand]
   You take down the office internet "for an emergency patch." The building goes
   quiet, then loud: ten people wander toward your desk holding their laptops
   like sick pets. Marcy, who lives in her inbox, comes down herself, and you tell
   her the router problem is on the fourth floor. Two minutes, maybe. You walk
   into Daniel's office. The keyboard is right there. Next to it, on paper (the
   internet is down), his retreat packing list, with "keyboard" underlined.
   Choice: "Back out. Think."

ACT 3: THE RETREAT  [cancel it with a fake sick grandmother]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. THE REALIZATION  [YOURS: need to cancel / PROPOSED: scene]
   You can't do this at a retreat, in the woods, with forty coworkers. The retreat
   has to be canceled. And Daniel is far too nice to cancel it himself... unless
   someone he cares about needs him. You don't have a grandmother.
   Choice: "Get a grandmother."

11. THE CASTING  [YOURS: pay someone to act as grandma; name: Lilly]
   You post an ad for a "small family role, elderly woman, $80 an hour, no
   questions." Three candidates. One is far too good. Her name is Lilly. You hire
   her.
   Choice: "Brief her."

12. THE BRIEFING  [PROPOSED]
   You tell Daniel your grandmother is very sick and you can't go to the retreat.
   He's stricken. He says the retreat is canceled "so nobody's left out." Then he
   asks where she is, because he'd like to visit.
   Choice: "Take him to see her."

13. THE VISIT  [PROPOSED]
   Daniel arrives with flowers and a casserole. Lilly, in a bed, in a rented room,
   is a method actor. She knows more about you than you do. Daniel prays with her.
   You watch her cry real tears.
   Choice: "Get him out of there."

14. DANIEL CANCELS  [PROPOSED; Lilly paid off]
   Daniel sends the email: "Family comes first. Retreat is canceled. Please keep
   her in your prayers. Something better is coming." Marcy replies-all with a
   heart. You pay Lilly in cash. She asks for a good review and leaves. She is not
   coming back. Nobody knows it was you. You feel a little sick.
   Choice: "Check your inbox."

ACT 4: THE COMPLICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
15. THE AUDIT  [PROPOSED, OPEN]
   Daniel, still worried about your "grandmother," throws himself into work: an
   asset audit. Every keyboard is tagged, and your decoy has no tag, so the swap
   will be caught the moment someone scans it.
   Choice: "Make a tag."

16. THE LABEL MAKER  [PROPOSED]
   11 p.m., the supply closet, a label printer, and a stack of blank tags. The
   night cleaner walks by. You have never worked so hard on anything.
   Choice: "Go home and sleep."

17. THE AUDITOR  [PROPOSED; male, per your version]
   A dry, stern outside auditor arrives with a scanner and a clipboard. He counts
   everything twice. He likes you, which is worse.
   Choice: "Give him the tour."

18. THE TOUR  [PROPOSED]
   You walk him through the building, steering him away from Daniel's office. He
   works his way toward it anyway, floor by floor. You have to get there first.
   Choice: "Get there first."

ACT 5: THE HEIST
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
19. THE SWAP  [PROPOSED]
   Daniel's office is empty. Marcy is elsewhere. You put the decoy on the desk and
   the real one in your hands, or nearly. Footsteps. The auditor is at the door.
   Choice: "Act natural."

20. THE SCAN  [PROPOSED]
   He scans the decoy. The forged tag beeps green. You almost celebrate. Then he
   stops mid-sentence and starts to wheeze.
   Choice: "Look at him."

21. THE CHOICE  [YOURS]
   He's on one knee, gasping. The keyboard is right there. For one second you
   think about the keyboard. Only one second.
   Choice: "Grab the keyboard."

22. THE SHOCK  [YOURS]
   You are shocked you even thought about it. You feel so guilty. You grab the
   keyboard and run anyway.
   Choice: "Run."

23. THE RUN  [YOURS]
   Down the hall, keyboard in your arms. Your foot hits something on the floor:
   an inhaler. You say the F-word. (Uncensored in the game? Your call.)
   Choice: "Go back."

24. THE LIE  [YOURS]
   You give him the inhaler and make up a lie: you saw him drop it ten seconds
   ago. He breathes. He believes you.
   Choice: "Help him up."

25. THE QUESTION  [YOURS]
   He sees the keyboard in your arms. "Why did you grab the keyboard?" You say
   you don't know. You were too busy thinking about saving his life. It's
   almost true. He's moved. He assumes you'd never have let him die over a
   keyboard.
   Choice: "Walk out."

ACT 6: THE LAUNCH  [NEW: the weird part, from almost-space]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
26. THE HERO  [YOURS: keyboard goes to space; $100M; he started the company on it / PROPOSED: rest]
   The auditor tells everyone. Daniel is so proud he cries. Then he announces the
   "something better" that replaces the retreat: the company just crossed $100
   million in revenue, and he started it on that very keyboard. To represent
   growth, he's sending it to the edge of space on a weather balloon. The keyboard
   you "rescued." You are the guest of honor.
   Choice: "Say yes."

27. THE FIELD  [PROPOSED]
   Launch day. A field, folding tables, matching shirts, a balloon consultant
   named Brett who has never seen a balloon fail. The keyboard is sealed in a
   foam box with a camera. You have one chance to take it back.
   Choice: "Get close."

28. THE LIFTOFF  [PROPOSED]
   The tether snaps early. Your jacket is clipped to the payload strap. The ground
   shrinks. Marcy is waving. Daniel is waving. Everyone is so happy.
   Choice: "Hold on."

29. THE ASCENT  [PROPOSED]
   Cloud. Cold. Quiet. The sky goes black and you can see the curve of the Earth.
   You've never been this high, and this is the closest you've ever come to
   feeling something, and it has nothing to do with the keyboard.
   Choice: "Look down."

30. THE DESCENT  [PROPOSED]
   The balloon pops. The parachute opens late. You land in a field two counties
   over, keyboard in your arms, in front of a very confused farmer.
   Choice: "Get up."

31. THE VAN  [PROPOSED]
   The whole company arrives in a van, cheering. Nobody suspects. You are the
   most beloved employee in company history. The camera footage is on the news.
   Choice: "Get in the van."

ACT 7: THE PAYOFF  [back to grounded reality]
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
32. THE GUILT  [YOURS: premise / PROPOSED: scene]
   You feel guilty and rationalize: the company wants happy employees. You are
   an employee. You are happy. Aren't you?
   Choice: "Go home."

33. THE KEYBOARD AT HOME  [PROPOSED]
   It's on your kitchen table. It's plugged in. No balloons, no cheering.
   Choice: "Type your name."

34. TYPING  [PROPOSED]
   It feels like a keyboard. It always did. What you felt in Daniel's office was
   never the keyboard.
   Choice: "Turn it off."

35. ENDING  [YOURS]
   You finally have the keyboard, and you're still bored with life. A keyboard
   doesn't fix anything. Ending scene; the game offers replay.

OPEN QUESTIONS
- Is a weather balloon the right "almost space," or do you want it stranger
  (a rocket, a private space startup, the CEO's cousin's spaceship)?
- Scene 29: the ascent is the closest the protagonist comes to feeling something,
  and it's not the keyboard. Does that fit the ending, or should the ending still
  say nothing helped?
- The weird ramps up in Act 6 only. Do you want it to start earlier (Lilly gets
  weirder, the audit gets weirder)?
- Brett the balloon consultant is a placeholder name.
- The keyboard is now the "founding keyboard" of a $100M company. Does that change
  how the ending should hit? (An ordinary keyboard that built an empire, and it
  still fixes nothing for you.)
- Does 35 scenes feel right, or should Act 2 (the near misses) be shorter?
- Scenes 21-22: single choice by design ("Grab the keyboard"), the guilt carried
  by the writing. Okay?
- Scene 23: inhaler (asthma), or another device? The F-word uncensored?

LATER (not now): diversions that rejoin the spine, e.g. the opening's "restart"
and "printer model" choices, and the earlier "take it now" idea (Daniel drops and
breaks the keyboard). None of these are designed yet.
